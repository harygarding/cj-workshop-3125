Stage 1 of 6

👁️ Visibility

Where and how do candidates discover you as an employer?

Opening prompt →

"When you last hired someone good, how did they first find out you were hiring?"

Q

Which job boards or platforms does your store currently post on — and why those ones?

Q

Do you ever ask new starters how they first heard about your vacancy?

Q

How complete is your Indeed employer profile — photos, description, company info?

Q

Do you use any offline methods: window signs, social media, employee referrals?

Q

If a candidate searches "jobs near me" in your town tonight, will your listing appear?

Q

Do you have a consistent process for when and how you post new vacancies?

Facilitator Guide — Stage 1 · Visibility
Why this stage matters

This is the very top of the hiring funnel. Before anyone can apply, they have to know you're hiring. Most franchisees dramatically underestimate how invisible they are — they rely on one platform and hope the right person happens to be searching that day. Spoiler: the best candidates usually aren't.

What to listen for

Vague answers like "we just post on Indeed". Push them to name every channel they use. The real gap surfaces when they honestly admit they don't know how their last hire actually found them. That moment of uncertainty is your opening.

The question to land

"If the best candidate in your area is fully employed and not job hunting today — what are you doing to get in front of them?" Visibility isn't passive. Most stores are waiting to be found instead of going out to find people.

💬 Thought for a Minute · Brand Consistency

Employer branding is like a dating app.

If a match looks great on the app but their photos, bio, and social profiles all tell a different story — that's an instant red flag. Candidates feel exactly the same way about your brand.

Stage 2 of 6

🏆 Brand Appeal

Once a candidate notices you, what makes them curious enough to find out more?

Opening prompt →

"If your job ad sat beside five other local jobs, why would someone click yours first?"

Q

What do your current job ads say about working for you — beyond just the role description?

Q

Do your listings include real photos of your team, store, or actual work environment?

Q

Have you checked your Indeed employer reviews lately — and have you responded to any?

Q

What is your genuine #1 selling point as a local employer right now?

Q

Does a candidate reading your listing know who they're actually applying to work for?

Q

If you lined up your ad alongside a competitor's, what would make a candidate choose yours?

Facilitator Guide — Stage 2 · Brand Appeal
Why this stage matters

Being visible gets you seen. Brand appeal is what makes someone stop scrolling and read your listing over the one next to it. At franchise level, most ads look identical — generic, untailored, with nothing that tells a candidate what it's actually like to work there.

What to listen for

"We use the standard template" is the answer you're listening for. It signals they've never thought about what makes their specific store a good place to work versus any other Domino's. That's the gap. Surface it gently — most people don't realise it until you ask.

The question to land

"A candidate is looking at your listing and three others side by side. Nothing in yours tells them anything specific about your store, your manager, or your team. Why would they choose you?" Let the silence do the work.

Live Activity · Brand Appeal

What Does AI Say About Your Store?

Job seekers are increasingly using AI to research employers before they even read a job ad. In 30 seconds, you can find out exactly what AI already knows — and says — about working for you.

📱
Step 1 — Take out your phone

Open ChatGPT, Perplexity, or do a Google search and look at the AI Overview at the top.

🔍
Step 2 — Ask the question

Type: "What's it like to work at [your company name]?" or "Is [company] a good employer?"

🗣️
Step 3 — Read it aloud

Share what comes back with the group. Is it accurate? Positive? Would it encourage someone to apply?

Q

Was what came back accurate — and would it encourage or put off a candidate?

Q

Where do you think the AI learned that information about your company?

Q

Did your job ads, Indeed reviews, or employer profile shape the answer?

Q

What would you want AI to say about working for you?

💡

AI tools learn from job boards, review platforms, and public content about your company. Your Indeed profile, job descriptions, and employer reviews directly shape what AI says about your store — and what candidates see before they ever click Apply.

Stage 3 of 6

💰 The Offer

What specifically convinces a candidate that applying is worth their time?

Opening prompt →

"What is the strongest reason a candidate should choose your role over another local job?"

✅ Strong EVP — what good looks like

"Paid weekly · free food every shift · hours built around your schedule · team leader pathway within 6 months"

❌ Weak EVP — what most stores say

"You'll receive a Domino's uniform and access to the employee discount scheme"

Q

Is your hourly rate clearly shown in the ad — and how does it compare to local competitors?

Q

Do you list all relevant benefits: free food, flexible shifts, weekly pay, uniform provided?

Q

Are hours, contracted shifts, and scheduling requirements clear before someone applies?

Q

Do you mention training, development, or any progression pathway in the listing?

Q

Is there anything in your listing that speaks to a candidate's life — flexibility for college, weekends off?

Q

Would a 20-year-old reading your job ad feel genuinely excited, or just neutral?

Facilitator Guide — Stage 3 · The Offer
Why this stage matters

This is where emotion takes over from logic. Pay matters — but at this level of the market it's rarely the deciding factor alone. Flexibility, the team, the manager, progression — these are the real reasons people say yes or no. Most stores aren't communicating any of it.

What to listen for

"We offer competitive pay" is the red flag — it means they haven't articulated their EVP at all. Push them: what would a current team member say is the best thing about working there? If they struggle to answer, so does their job ad. That's the gap.

The question to land

"If I called one of your team members right now and asked why they stay, what would they say? And is any of that in your job listing?" That gap between what they'd say and what's written down is exactly what candidates are missing.

Stage 4 of 6

📋 Getting Applications

How easy and frictionless is it for someone to actually apply?

📱 Indeed data: 87% of hospitality job searches happen on a mobile phone — is your apply process ready for that?
Opening prompt →

"When was the last time you applied for one of your own jobs on a mobile phone?"

Q

How many steps does your application process involve from clicking "Apply" to submitting?

Q

Have you personally tried applying to your own listing on a mobile phone recently?

Q

Do you ask for a full CV for entry-level crew roles — and is that really necessary?

Q

Are there friction points: mandatory account creation, long forms, or eligibility tests?

Q

Does a candidate know upfront what information they'll need before they start applying?

Q

Could a motivated person complete your application in under 3 minutes on their phone?

Facilitator Guide — Stage 4 · Getting Applications
Why this stage matters

Friction kills conversion. A brilliant listing with a painful apply process will silently lose candidates at the very last step — and you'll never know they were there. At this market level, most people apply on a mobile during a break. They won't fight a clunky form.

What to listen for

Any mention of requiring a full CV for an entry-level role, multi-step forms, or mandatory account creation. The real tell: they've never actually tested their own application on a mobile phone. If that's the case, they're losing people they'll never know about.

The question to land

"Take your phone out and start an application for your own vacancy right now. How far do you get before it gets frustrating?" This exercise in the room is more powerful than any stat — they feel what candidates feel.

Stage 5 of 6

⚡ Responding & Screening

What happens after someone hits apply — and how quickly does it happen?

⏱️ Candidates who don't hear back within 24 hours are 3× more likely to accept a competitor's offer first
Opening prompt →

"How quickly does a good applicant hear from you after they apply?"

Q

What is your typical response time after a new application arrives — hours or days?

Q

Does every applicant receive an automatic acknowledgement the moment they apply?

Q

Who in your store owns the job of reviewing and responding to new applications?

Q

What does your interview invite process look like — phone call, text message, or email?

Q

Do you send a reminder message to candidates the day before their interview slot?

Q

What is your interview no-show rate — and what's your honest view on what's causing it?

Facilitator Guide — Stage 5 · Responding & Screening
Why this stage matters

Hourly workers apply to multiple roles at once. Speed is the entire game here — the employer who responds first gets first pick. Waiting 3–5 days in this market is effectively saying no. Most no-shows at interview aren't rude candidates; they're candidates who already accepted somewhere else.

What to listen for

"We try to get back within a few days" is the red flag. Also watch for ambiguity about who actually owns the task of responding — if it's vague, it means applications are sitting unread. That's a process problem disguised as a candidate problem.

The question to land

"Think of the strongest candidate you missed in the last few months. What was your response time? And do you know who hired them instead?" This makes the cost of slowness personal and real, not theoretical.

Stage 6 of 6

🎉 Making the Hire

What happens at the point of offer — and does it set the relationship up for success?

Opening prompt →

"Where do candidates drop out after you have spoken to them — interview, offer, paperwork, first shift or first week?"

Q

How do you deliver a job offer — phone call, text, email — and how quickly after interview?

Q

Is all onboarding paperwork completed before the new starter's first shift?

Q

Does a new hire know exactly what to expect on day one before they even arrive?

Q

Do you have a buddy or welcome system to support people through their first week?

Q

What happens if a new team member has a difficult or confusing first shift?

Q

How many new starters leave within the first four weeks — and what is the main reason?

Facilitator Guide — Stage 6 · Making the Hire
Why this stage matters

Closing the deal is not just about making the offer. The candidate is deciding on you right up until their first shift. A slow offer, a confusing first day, or a missing welcome can unravel everything you worked for across the previous five stages.

What to listen for

High early attrition — "we get a lot of people who don't come back after the first shift" — is almost always a sign of a broken onboarding experience, not bad candidates. Push them to describe what actually happens between offer and day one. The gap is usually obvious.

The question to land

"What does a new starter know about working for you after their interview that they didn't know before they walked in? And what happens in their first hour on shift?" If the answers are vague, the experience is vague — and that's what's costing you retention.
